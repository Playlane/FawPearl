How can therapy and medication help with depression?

Depression is more than feeling sad. It can drain your energy, change your sleep and appetite, and make it hard to enjoy anything. It is one of the most common health conditions, and one of the most treatable.

We'll take time to understand what you're going through and what may be contributing to it. Together we'll choose a plan, which may include talk therapy, medication or both, and we'll keep adjusting until you start to feel like yourself again.

  • Medication management. Antidepressants can help many people. We'll explain your options, start carefully and follow up to see how you're responding.
  • Talk therapy. Work through the thoughts, habits and life stresses that keep depression going, and rebuild routines that lift your mood.
  • Both together. For moderate to severe depression, therapy and medication together often work better than either alone.

Symptoms

Common signs of depression

Depression affects people in different ways. Signs can include:

In your body

  • Feeling tired or slowed down most days
  • Sleeping too much or too little
  • Changes in appetite or weight
  • Aches and pains without a clear cause

In your thoughts and feelings

  • Sadness, emptiness or tearfulness
  • Losing interest in things you used to enjoy
  • Feeling guilty, worthless or hopeless
  • Trouble concentrating or making decisions

Only a qualified clinician can make a diagnosis. This page is general information, not medical advice.

How do I know if it's depression?

If low mood, loss of interest or the signs above last most days for two weeks or more, it's worth getting an evaluation.

How long does treatment take to work?

Many people feel some improvement within a few weeks of starting therapy or medication. We'll check in regularly and adjust the plan if needed.

Can I do therapy without medication?

Yes. Therapy alone can work well, especially for mild to moderate depression. We'll talk about what makes sense for you.

What if I'm having thoughts of suicide?

Please get help now: call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or call 911. Our online visits are not an emergency service.
